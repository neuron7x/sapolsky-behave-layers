## K0 — continuous conductance bound
Assume scalar target conductance g>=0, between positive impulsive arrivals dg/dt=-g/6 ms, each arrival adds an integer multiple of .5 nS. Target [.4,.6]. For each connected band visit the duration is at most D=6 ln(3/2): any positive jump from the band overshoots .6; free decay traverses the band in at most D. If leaving through an upward jump, g>=.9, so next entry cannot occur sooner than D even with additional positive impulses. If leaving through lower boundary .4, re-entry via a jump requires pre-jump g<=.1 and hence elapsed time >=6 ln(4)>D; re-entry via decay from above after at least one jump has g(t)>=.9 exp(-t/6), so elapsed time >=D. Endpoint membership is a zero-measure convention; exclude Zeno/infinitely many arrivals in a finite time. Therefore a finite horizon T with n nonzero-duration visits has occupancy time <= min(nD,T-(n-1)D), and long-run fraction <=1/2. Compute conservative exact rational upper bounds using a 40-term positive atanh series with geometric tail for ln(3/2); use upper D for dwell and lower D for gap. Horizons 12.8,38.4,51.2,320 ms. Check an independent alternating-block envelope and rational witness cases; no claim that upper bound is achievable by the neural plant. At a route with zero connected source group, g(t)=g(0)exp(-t/6) independent of every source current, hence no controllability of this conductance target in that route.
